Split tunneling apps for Mac, compared

macOS can't send one app through a VPN on its own, so a few small apps fill the gap. They come in two shapes. SplitTunnel and AppLanes sit alongside the VPN app you already run and decide, app by app, whether traffic goes through it, around it, or nowhere. VPNonly replaces the VPN app: it connects to your provider itself, and only the apps you switch on ever use the tunnel.

I make VPNonly, so read this with that in mind. Everything below about the other two comes from their own websites, checked on 13 November 2026.

Which one fits

  • You use a work VPN such as GlobalProtect or Cisco AnyConnect: SplitTunnel or AppLanes. They work with the client your company gave you. VPNonly needs a WireGuard config or a NordVPN token, and it won't run alongside a VPN that covers the whole Mac.
  • You need Safari on the VPN: VPNonly can't do it, because WebKit hands its connections to system processes that don't carry Safari's identity. Check with the others before you pay.
  • You'd rather not subscribe: VPNonly is $19 once. Over two years at their yearly prices, SplitTunnel comes to $98 and AppLanes to $39.98.
  • You're on NordVPN: Nord's Mac app has no split tunneling at all. VPNonly signs in with an access token and gives you every Nord country, with Nord's app closed. How that works.
  • You want to read the code that runs as root: VPNonly's privileged engine is published, and the command-line version is free and MIT licensed.
  • You want something Apple has reviewed: AppLanes is on the Mac App Store. VPNonly isn't signed by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to allow it once in System Settings.

What about your VPN's own app?

Several providers now build split tunneling into their Mac apps, but most can only leave apps out of a full tunnel, and NordVPN's can't do either. If leaving apps out is what you want, use the one you already pay for. Which Mac VPNs have split tunneling goes provider by provider.

What VPNonly can't do

Besides Safari: DNS lookups go through your normal resolver, not the tunnel, so your ISP can still see which sites you look up. An app relaunches once when it joins the VPN, because what VPNonly routes by is fixed when an app starts. And there's no Windows version.
