Why this exists

Commercial VPN apps on macOS are all-or-nothing: connect, and your whole machine goes through the tunnel — video calls get slower, banking sites get suspicious, everything routes through another country. Split tunneling fixes that, but no major provider ships it on macOS.

VPNonly flips the model: your Mac stays on your normal connection, and only the apps you pick go through the VPN. It never touches your default route, so the worst-case failure mode is "that one app is offline" — never "my Mac lost internet." A built-in kill switch blocks a chosen app's traffic if the tunnel drops, instead of leaking it.

FAQ

How does it work?

A userspace WireGuard tunnel is brought up with no default route, so nothing uses it by default. macOS's built-in PF firewall then steers traffic from the apps you choose into the tunnel. The engine is open source — you can read every line that runs with elevated privileges.

Which VPN providers work?

NordVPN works out of the box (it fetches your WireGuard credentials with an access token from your Nord account). Any provider that gives you a WireGuard config also works: Mullvad, Proton, IVPN, or your own server.

Is my traffic safe if the VPN drops?

Yes — a kill switch blocks the chosen apps' traffic entirely if the tunnel goes down. It never silently falls back to your normal connection.

Known limitations?

v1 is Apple Silicon only. DNS queries for tunneled apps currently use the system resolver (connections themselves are tunneled) — fine for changing your exit IP, and a fix is on the roadmap. Apps must be launched via VPNonly to be inside the tunnel.
