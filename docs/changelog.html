Changelog

Everything that's changed, including the things that were broken. VPNonly updates itself from 1.5.0 onwards.

1.9.15 · 25 August 2026

  • Turning an app off no longer asks to quit it, because it never needed to be quit.

1.9.8 – 1.9.14 · August 2026

  • Works when another VPN already holds a tunnel interface. VPNonly used to assume one was its own, and could report a connection it didn't have.
  • Its firewall rules live in their own space, so they no longer disturb other tools' rules.
  • "Connected" only appears once the VPN server has actually answered.
  • Asks before closing a running app. A background check could previously quit one with no warning.
  • A blocked app fails immediately instead of hanging until it times out.
  • Tries another server when one doesn't answer.
  • Simpler setup, with clear instructions if a much older version is still installed.
  • No longer shows a problem while it's still working.

1.9.0 – 1.9.7 · August 2026

  • Check. Compares a routed app's exit IP with your Mac's own, and re-checks in the background.
  • Switching an app out is instant. It used to keep using the tunnel for up to a minute.
  • Safari removed from the list. No per-app VPN on macOS can route it.
  • Release This Mac, to move a licence to another machine.
  • Open it in a window instead of the menu bar panel, and it remembers which you prefer.
  • Updates arrive within the hour.
  • Fixes for launching after a reinstall, and for apps appearing closed after an update.

1.5.0 – 1.6.5 · August 2026

  • Every country your provider offers, 149 rather than a hardcoded ten, with search and recents.
  • Apps stay in the VPN after you quit them. The switch is a lasting choice.
  • Automatic updates, without macOS asking you to approve the app again.
  • Opens in a window with a Dock icon when the menu bar is full.
  • Multi-monitor and licence field fixes.

1.2.0 – 1.4.0 · August 2026

  • Any WireGuard provider: import a config from Mullvad, Proton, IVPN, AirVPN or your own server, alongside NordVPN token setup.
  • Instant switching. Each app gets its own group, so moving one in or out is a firewall change rather than a restart.
  • The menu bar app: every app on your Mac, searchable, with a switch each.
  • One password prompt ever, instead of one per action.
  • Licence activation, and an uninstaller that removes the root engine and its permissions.
